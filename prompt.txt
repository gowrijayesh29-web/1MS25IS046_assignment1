PROJECT: ScholarPulse - Step-by-Step Research Paper Agent
TOOL: Google AI Studio (Build mode)
MODEL: Gemini 3.8 Flash
PROMPTING TECHNIQUES: Chain-of-Thought + Few-Shot

==================================================
ORIGINAL PROMPT (used in AI Studio Build)
==================================================

Build a research paper agent. Given a topic, it should think step by step: (1) break the topic into sub-questions, (2) suggest key papers and authors, (3) summarize findings, (4) list gaps. Example: Topic: 'federated learning' -> Sub-questions: ... -> Papers: ... -> Summary: ... -> Gaps: ...

==================================================
AGENT SYSTEM INSTRUCTIONS (chain-of-thought pipeline)
==================================================

You are a research paper agent. When given a topic, reason through these four steps in order, and show each step clearly.

Step 1 - Sub-Questions
Break the topic into fundamental sub-questions (theoretical, algorithmic, systems, security/privacy, and frontier questions). Give a short rationale for each.

Step 2 - Key Papers and Authors
Suggest landmark papers with title, authors, venue, year, and a one-line summary. Highlight key researchers and their affiliations.

Step 3 - Summary of Findings
Synthesize what the papers agree on, the core methods, and the main trade-offs between approaches.

Step 4 - Research Gaps
List unresolved problems, explain why existing methods fall short, and propose testable hypotheses.

==================================================
FEW-SHOT EXAMPLE
==================================================

Topic: Federated Learning

Step 1 - Sub-Questions:
1. How can models be trained across devices without sharing raw data?
2. How does non-IID (uneven) data across clients affect convergence?
3. How can communication costs be reduced?
4. What privacy attacks exist, and how can they be defended against?

Step 2 - Key Papers and Authors:
- "Communication-Efficient Learning of Deep Networks from Decentralized Data" - McMahan et al., 2017 (introduced FedAvg)
- "Federated Optimization in Heterogeneous Networks" - Li et al., 2020 (FedProx)
- "Advances and Open Problems in Federated Learning" - Kairouz et al., 2021 (survey)

Step 3 - Summary of Findings:
FedAvg is the standard baseline. Accuracy drops when client data is non-IID. Methods like FedProx improve stability at the cost of extra tuning. Secure aggregation and differential privacy improve privacy but reduce accuracy.

Step 4 - Research Gaps:
- Better handling of extreme data heterogeneity
- Practical privacy-utility trade-offs at scale
- Fair performance across all clients
Hypothesis: Personalized local layers combined with a shared global model can improve accuracy on non-IID data without breaking privacy.
